# H₂S Wristband --- SbCl₃ + Purple-Cabbage Anthocyanin

## 1. Scope and safety

This is the **laboratory benchmark version** of the wristband, based on
*A Visual Color Response Test Paper for the Detection of Hydrogen
Sulfide Gas in the Air*, Molecules 2023, 28, 5044.

The paper tested SbCl₃ concentrations of 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.50 and 1.00
wt% and anthocyanin preparations of 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 wt%. It selected
**0.5 wt% SbCl₃ + 4 wt% anthocyanin preparation** for subsequent work.
The published composite reported a 200 ppb detection limit and useful
ΔE/concentration behavior in approximately the 1--10 ppm range. Those
are published results, not guaranteed results for a homemade batch.

**Important:** SbCl₃ is not a skin-contact material. Treat this
formulation as a laboratory benchmark in a replaceable cartridge,
physically separated from skin. Prepare it only under appropriate
laboratory supervision, PPE and fume extraction.

H₂S exposure testing must use an approved, monitored system with
certified gas/calibration equipment. Do not improvise an acid+sulfide
H₂S generator.

## 4. Anthocyanin extraction

### Original paper procedure

The paper dried purple cabbage at **60 °C for 36 h**, ground it, soaked
it in 1:1 ethanol/water for **2 h**, sonicated for **30 min at 30 °C and
200 W**, then centrifuged at **3500 rpm, 10 °C for 15 min** and
collected the supernatant.

### Your current fresh-cabbage batch

You are using **100 g fresh cabbage + 200 mL of 50% ethanol/water**.
This is a modified fresh-cabbage method, not identical to the paper's
dried-cabbage method.

1.  Wash 100 g fresh purple cabbage.
2.  Cut into small pieces.
3.  Add 200 mL total 50:50 ethanol/water.
4.  Cover the vessel and protect it from strong light.
5.  Stir periodically.
6.  Use a standardized room-temperature extraction period; **6 h** is a
    practical fresh-cabbage protocol.
7.  Filter out the cabbage.
8.  If available, centrifuge the filtered liquid at **3500 rpm, 10 °C,
    15 min**.
9.  Collect the clear purple supernatant.
10. Store closed in an amber/dark bottle at about 4 °C.

Do not call this extract "4 wt% anthocyanin" unless you actually
quantify anthocyanin. Use the same extraction batch and volume for all
comparison strips.

## 5. SbCl₃ formulation screening

The paper prepared SbCl₃ solutions by dissolving the following masses in
**10 g of 1:1 ethanol/water**:

      SbCl₃   Approx. concentration
  --------- -----------------------
    0.005 g                0.05 wt%
    0.010 g                0.10 wt%
    0.020 g                0.20 wt%
    0.050 g                0.50 wt%
    0.100 g                1.00 wt%

The paper ultimately selected the **0.5 wt% SbCl₃ + 4 wt% anthocyanin
preparation** combination for further testing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Prepare the mixed indicator

For the paper's 0.5 wt% SbCl₃ formulation, calculate SbCl₃ as **0.005 ×
the mass of anthocyanin solution**.

Example:

-   anthocyanin solution = 10.00 g
-   SbCl₃ required for 0.5 wt% = 0.050 g

Procedure:

1.  Weigh the anthocyanin solution.
2.  Calculate 0.5% of its mass.
3.  Weigh that amount of SbCl₃.
4.  Add under appropriate laboratory controls.
5.  Stir for **10 min**.
6.  Use the mixture for coating.

The "4 wt% anthocyanin" designation in the paper refers to its
experimental anthocyanin preparation level; it is not a measurement of
purified anthocyanin in your fresh extract.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Make the sensing paper

The paper used **2 cm × 5 cm** filter paper.

For each strip:

1.  Cut identical paper pieces.
2.  Apply **0.5 mL** of mixed indicator.
3.  Dry at approximately **25 °C**.
4.  Apply another **0.5 mL**.
5.  Dry completely.

Total coating = **1.0 mL/strip**.

Make at least three replicates.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Controls

Make:

-   **C0:** untreated paper
-   **C1:** anthocyanin only
-   **C2:** SbCl₃ only
-   **T1:** 0.5 wt% SbCl₃ + selected anthocyanin preparation

This reproduces the paper's important comparison between
anthocyanin-only, SbCl₃-only and mixed strips.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. H₂S testing

The paper used a sealed glass-container arrangement and an H₂S sensor to
verify concentration. For a student project, use a **certified H₂S
calibration gas, controlled exposure chamber, continuous H₂S monitor and
appropriate exhaust/abatement** rather than improvising gas generation.

For each strip:

1.  Photograph before exposure.
2.  Put the strip in the approved chamber.
3.  Verify actual H₂S concentration with the monitor.
4.  Record the exposure time.
5.  Photograph at fixed intervals.
6.  Keep camera, distance, lighting and background constant.

For your long-duration study, record:

**0 h → 1 h → 2 h → 4 h → 6 h → 8 h**

The actual 8-hour concentration must be selected and approved by your
laboratory supervisor.

## 10. Colour calculation

Convert the image to CIE Lab and calculate:

**ΔE = √\[(L*−L0*)² + (a*−a0*)² + (b*−b0*)²\]**

where the subscript 0 values are the pre-exposure values.

Build your own calibration curve using your actual strips and camera. Do
not copy the paper's calibration equation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Wristband construction

### Recommended structure

**Reusable strap + replaceable sensor cartridge**

The SbCl₃ strip must not directly touch the skin.

### Sustainable strap choices

1.  Upcycled event/polyester wristbands
2.  Recycled PET/polyester webbing
3.  Recycled cotton webbing
4.  Hemp/cotton webbing

For harsh industrial handling, recycled polyester is the practical
choice.

### Cartridge

the prototype, or another mechanically stable material.

## 14. Seven-day expiry patch

Use a separate cellulose patch, not SbCl₃.

A sustainable option is a **cellulose + anthocyanin time indicator**.

Calibrate it rather than assuming it lasts exactly seven days.

Prepare at least 15 identical patches and measure colour on:

**Day 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8**

Calculate ΔE relative to Day 0.

Use the experimentally determined threshold to show "EXPIRED".

Best design:

-   chemical patch = visual indication
-   QR expiry date = authoritative validity check

This prevents temperature/humidity variations from making the chemical
patch the sole expiry mechanism.

## 17. What you can safely claim

The paper supports the statement that its SbCl₃/anthocyanin composite
produced a strong visual H₂S response and reported a 200 ppb detection
limit.

For your own project, do not claim:

-   200 ppb detection;
-   a particular ppm calibration;
-   8-hour exposure accuracy;
-   skin safety;
-   exactly seven-day chemical expiry

until your own experiments establish them.

The strongest positioning is:

**SbCl₃ formulation = laboratory performance benchmark.**

**SbCl₃-free formulation = candidate final wearable.**
